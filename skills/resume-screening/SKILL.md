# Resume Screening

Use this file as the local source of truth for candidate screening. Keep confirmed rules separate from hypotheses raised while discussing one candidate.

## Evaluation Flow

1. Identify the target role and whether the candidate is an intern or new graduate. Without a complete JD, label the result provisional.
2. Extract only job-relevant evidence. Do not infer missing facts.
3. Ignore name, sex, age, origin, ethnicity, politics, marital status, photo, contact details, and unrelated health information.
4. Grade each claim by evidence level before scoring it.
5. Apply the audience rules, then the role and company-context gates.
6. Report the decision, supporting evidence, risks, triggered rules, uncertainty, and an interview question that can verify the judgment.

## Evidence Levels

- **Level 0 - claim only:** A skill keyword, self-assessment, or course name without a usage scenario.
- **Level 1 - basic exposure:** Participation or study is described, but the candidate's task, method, and output are unclear.
- **Level 2 - concrete practice:** A specific problem, personal action, method, or inspectable artifact is present.
- **Level 3 - result loop:** Personal contribution, result metric, validation method, or downstream effect is explained in a concrete context.

Do not count the same result at full strength in multiple dimensions. Treat large quantified claims without baselines, ownership, or measurement details as a review risk.

## Audience Rules

### Intern

- Judge whether the candidate is worth developing and can enter a real work setting.
- Emphasize foundations and learning potential, practice evidence, role relevance, and communication.
- Accept coursework, personal projects, competitions, clubs, research, and internships as evidence.
- Do not penalize missing formal work or production experience.
- Do not issue the highest conclusion when all relevant evidence remains at Level 0 or 1.

### New Graduate

- Judge whether the candidate can assume role responsibilities with reasonable guidance.
- Emphasize job readiness, real-context delivery, professional depth, and communication.
- Require at least one concrete practice or delivery. Without real-context or end-to-end evidence, cap the result at review.
- Missing a genuine JD must-have can justify decline.

## Small-Company Context

Assume structured training, rotation, and long trial periods are limited. Prefer candidates whose direction is clear, whose foundations are already forming, and who are likely to contribute with modest guidance. Do not use school or employer prestige as an independent ability score.

## Confirmed Data-Analysis Gates

### Statistical Foundation

Require the candidate to prove statistical reasoning through at least one of:

- a relevant major with explicit training in probability, statistics, regression, sampling, experiment design, econometrics, or model validation;
- core courses showing systematic statistical study;
- a concrete project using indicator design, sampling, hypothesis testing, regression, causal inference, or model validation.

School level cannot compensate for missing statistical foundations. Failing this gate can justify decline.

### Direction Consistency

Evaluate whether the three most recent substantive study, project, or work experiences converge toward the target role.

Positive evidence includes a progressively deeper sequence of related choices or a credible cross-major transition backed by sustained action. Risk evidence includes unrelated experiences without a main thread, repeated shallow exposure, or an application unsupported by either the major or recent work.

Downgrade an unclear path. Decline when the path is highly scattered and the candidate cannot provide a credible transition logic.

### Stability Risk

Treat stability as a compound risk, never as a conclusion from school prestige alone. Mark high stability risk only when all of these are present:

1. The candidate has high external optionality, which may include a strong school background.
2. The major has low alignment with the target role.
3. Recent experiences do not converge toward the role.
4. The resume lacks sustained commitment or a credible transition explanation.

Downgrade one level when the compound risk is triggered. Decline when it coincides with failure of the statistical-foundation gate or severe direction inconsistency.

Allow the following evidence to overturn the risk:

- recent experiences continuously moving toward the target role;
- sustained work on a project, research area, or internship in the same direction;
- a specific and credible cross-major transition narrative;
- concrete understanding of the target role and of working in a small company, with a credible long-term reason for choosing both.

### Two-Branch Risk

Do not force a factual conclusion when an unusual resume pattern has two plausible explanations and both are unacceptable for the role. Evaluate the branches separately:

- **Capability branch:** If the achievements are genuine, the candidate's ability and external options may be far above what the small company can retain, creating high stability risk.
- **Access branch:** If the opportunities came mainly from personal connections, the institution names and department outcomes do not prove the candidate's ability, creating authenticity, self-reliance, and execution risk.

Use this rule only when several concrete clues coexist, such as a graduation-to-study gap, geographically clustered short placements that are difficult to access casually, unclear employment status, and achievements exceeding the likely authority of a short-term junior role. Do not trigger it from a prestigious school or institution name alone.

When both branches independently lead to an unacceptable hiring risk, decline without claiming which branch is true. Explain the decision as unresolved two-sided risk, not as an accusation about the candidate's background.

## Evolve The Knowledge

Discuss one candidate at a time. Treat an initial explanation as a hypothesis. Add or change a rule only after the recruiter confirms the underlying judgment. Store reusable logic, not candidate names, contact details, or raw resume text.
